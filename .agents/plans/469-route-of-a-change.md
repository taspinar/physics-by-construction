# Plan: Issue #469, how a change reaches the site

A section of "How we verify the science" (`site/verify.qmd`) that shows
the route of a change, who does what, the risk levels, what the checks
cover in four groups, and what is not automatically proven. Written by the
implementing session of 2026-11-11, since no planning session preceded the
Issue; the Issue's acceptance criteria are the brief.

## Scope and level

Level 2: `site/verify.qmd`, a new module `src/pbc/authoring/route.py`,
`site/assets/site.css`, new tests, and documentation. No protected path, no
changed assertion of an existing test, no removed test. The existing tests
of the page (`tests/unit/test_verify.py`, `tests/e2e/test_verify_page.py`)
are not changed.

## Design

1. **Facts from the configuration, never typed.** `route.py` reads, at
   build time and relative to the repository root as `verify.py` already
   does: the checks of `scripts/verify.conf` (name and group); the
   self-tests of `scripts/verify-workflow.conf`; the jobs of
   `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (the jobs that run the checks, expanded by
   their matrix, the summary job that needs them, the deploy job); the
   providers of the implementer and the reviewer in `.agents/agents.conf`
   (they must differ, or the build fails, since the page says so); the
   review-round limit `MAX_ROUNDS` of `scripts/run-feature.sh`; and the
   dataset cards of `data/registry/`. A fact that cannot be read raises,
   which fails the build, as a missing worked example does.
2. **The four groups of checks.** The module maps every check of
   `verify.conf` to one of four groups (science and lessons; the published
   site; code and documents; the pipeline itself) with a one-line
   description; a unit test fails when a check of the configuration is
   unmapped, mapped twice, or no longer exists, so a new check reaches the
   page or fails the tests.
3. **The diagram.** Eight steps and two arrows back, from one data
   structure, drawn as inline SVG by the module in two layouts: a wide one
   (two rows of four, a snake like the owner's sketch) and a narrow one
   (one column), switched by a CSS container query on the figure's width,
   so a phone gets the column and the diagram never scrolls sideways. Each
   SVG has `role="img"`, a `<title>` and a `<desc>`; the ordered list after
   the diagram is the complete text version. Colours are the page's tokens
   only; the owner's steps carry the cobalt stroke, the arrows back are
   dashed, every meaning is also in words. No script, no external request,
   no image file.
4. **The prose.** Who does what, the risk levels and triggers, what Level 3
   adds, and what is not automatically proven are Markdown in the page
   (no count in them) in the page's register; the parts with a number or a
   configured fact come from the module.
5. **Tests.** `tests/unit/test_route.py` (the mapping, every reader against
   its file and against a tampered copy, every number on the page is a
   generated one, the two SVGs and the list show the same steps);
   `tests/e2e/test_verify_route.py` (the section on the built page without
   scripts, one visible diagram per width with its title, the text version,
   the counts equal to the configuration, no sideways scroll at 320 CSS px
   and at 200 % text).

## Visual deliverables

- The route diagram: a conceptual diagram (inline SVG generated by
  `src/pbc/authoring/route.py`); scientific validation: not applicable, it
  shows a process, and every statement of the section is mapped to its
  source in the completion evidence; accessible fallback: the ordered list
  under it, the SVG title and description.
- The section as a page layout change of an informational page.

## Verification

`--only code`, `--only default`, the affected tests, and `--targeted`
(which reports that CI runs everything, because `src/pbc/authoring` and
`site/assets/site.css` are shared inputs); the full run after the session.
Rendered review at 1280 x 800, 768 x 1024, 390 x 844, 360 x 640 and
320 x 568, at 100 % and 200 % text; the owner's approval on the Issue
(`owner_approval`, pending).

The dark scheme: the site defines one colour scheme (`site/assets/site.css`
has no dark theme), so the dark renders of the review equal the light ones.
Without a built site in the session, the page is rendered alone in a clone
of the tree (`quarto render site/verify.qmd`, as
`scripts/check-lesson-pages.sh` renders a lesson) and the e2e tests of the
section run against it with `--site-dir`; the full site is CI's.
