# Dossier: the F145 bench sessions, series RLC and LED-pair campaigns on an Analog Discovery 3

Card: `data/registry/ad3-bench-2026-11.yaml`. Recorded 2026-11-27 for F145 part 3 (Issue #614): the datasets of the owner's two bench sessions with the Autonomous Physics Lab (`pbc.products.physics_lab`). The owner's decisions for this part are on the Issue ([2026-11-27](https://github.com/taspinar/physics-by-construction/issues/614#issuecomment-7210910945)).

**Decision: go.** Kind: the exports of the project's own live campaigns: run records, measurement tables, evidence logs, and the raw samples of three campaigns. Every campaign of both sessions is published, with its failed checks: 27 on the series RLC circuit and 2 on the LED pair. The measurements are the owner's, published under CC BY 4.0 by the owner's decisions of [2026-11-25](https://github.com/taspinar/physics-by-construction/issues/614#issuecomment-7173989078) (the series RLC runs of bench session 1, which the supervisor read as covering the runs of 2026-11-26), [2026-11-26](https://github.com/taspinar/physics-by-construction/issues/614#issuecomment-7197996080) (both LED runs, with the failed model validation and all raw data), and [2026-11-27](https://github.com/taspinar/physics-by-construction/issues/614#issuecomment-7210910945) (all 27 RLC campaigns and both LED runs, nothing left out).

This dossier states what was measured and what the product computed. It assigns no cause to any difference between campaigns, between the two protocols, or with the pilot.

## The device identifier

The device's serial number is replaced in every published file, in the repository and in the archive, by the stable identifier **AD3-001**. The original files stay unchanged on the owner's machine. The serial occurs in two fields of each export, `run.json` `run.device.serial` and `checkpoints/checkpoint.json` `bench.serial`. The packager replaces those fields as the declaration names them. It never searches for a literal value: the value to remove is read from the input. Afterwards it searches every output file, inside the archive too, for the removed value, and refuses the package on a hit. Each JSON file is written back with the indentation it had, so only the field changes (`MANIFEST.tsv` lists every changed file with the original's SHA-256 beside the published one).

Digests recorded *inside* the files refer to the original files and were not rewritten. These are `run.json` `segment.checkpoint_digest` (the original `checkpoints/checkpoint.json`) and the SHA-256 of `raw.csv` in `evidence.json` (the complete original `raw.csv`). The published pilot dataset `ad3-rlc-pilot` keeps its serial for now (the owner's decision 5).

What the table shows, stated without a cause:

- **The two protocols on Q.** The ring-downs give a higher Q than the frequency response in every campaign, by 10.4 to 13.0 combined standard errors. The evaluator's verdict is therefore false in all 27 campaigns.
- **The two protocols on f0.** They agree within two combined standard errors in 25 of 27 campaigns. They differ in `2026-11-25-series-rlc` (2.91) and `2026-11-26-series-rlc-run16` (2.14).
- **The model's fit.** The reduced chi-square of the frequency-response fit is 3,450 to 5,500 in every campaign: the second-order model does not describe the points within their noise. Its standard errors understate the spread of f0 and Q.
- **Within the session of 2026-11-26.** The −90° crossing was 49.99 kHz at 5 minutes after plug-in. It was 49.52 to 49.58 kHz in the ten campaigns at about 8 minutes, 48.87 kHz at 27 minutes, and lowest at 48.46 kHz at 43 minutes. - **Between the sessions.** On 2026-11-25 the crossing fell from 50.18 to 49.67 kHz over three campaigns in eight minutes.

## The LED pair

Two runs, six minutes apart (16:07:48 and 16:13:47 UTC), on the same breadboard with the same plan: 13 amplitudes at 1 kHz, every one valid.

| Run | Gain at 2.0 V | Third harmonic at 2.0 V | Fitted ideality n (± 1 s.e.) | Voltage at 1 mA (± 1 s.e.) | Held-out check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `2026-11-26-led-pair` | 0.973 | 2.0 % | 50.9 ± 5.5 | 1.740 ± 0.029 V | fails |
| `2026-11-26-led-pair-run2` | 0.973 | 2.0 % | 2.32 ± 0.034 | 1.8554 ± 0.0003 V | fails |

The reports give the intervals at ± 2 s.e. (n = 50.9 ± 11.0 and 2.32 ± 0.07). The gains of the two runs differ by at most 0.0043 and the third harmonics by at most 0.0022 (the figure), yet the fitted characteristic differs by more than a factor of 20 in n. The characteristic fitted at 2.0 V predicts the gain at every other amplitude. That prediction misses the measured gain beyond two combined standard errors at 11 of the 12 other amplitudes in run 1 and at all 12 in run 2. The evaluator's verdict is false in both runs.

Known limits of these measurements, stated and not resolved here:

- Channel 2 sat on the 50 V range, so a few hundred millivolts across the series resistor are read near the bottom of that range.
- The mean of channel 2 over each acquisition's 513 samples is −47.8 to −42.0 mV in run 1 and −16.7 to −5.2 mV in run 2 (this dossier's reading). These offsets differ between two runs six minutes apart, and the characteristic is fitted without a verified zero.
- The characteristic is fitted to the samples above 0.5 mA: 593 samples in run 1 and 297 in run 2.

The follow-up is Issue #633 (fitting ranges, a verified zero, linear references, a pre-check). It is named here and not anticipated.

## Uncertainty and calibration

- **Per acquisition.** The product's standard errors: the sine fits per point (frequency response), the scatter of the ring-downs with the Birge ratio, the LED characteristic's fit.
- **The model.** The frequency-response fit's reduced chi-square of 3,450 to 5,500 means its standard errors of f0 and Q do not cover the misfit.
- **The device.** The factory calibration and the channels' relative gain and phase on their two ranges are unknown and not included.
- **The parts.** Nominal values; none was measured.

## The CI replay

The product's recorded adapter replays three exports in the build, with no device, no SDK, and no network (`tests/unit/test_data_ad3_bench_2026_11.py`):

- `2026-11-26-series-rlc`, its 31 frequency-response records, with the plan narrowed to the frequency-response protocol. The adaptive route measures that protocol first, so the replay asks for the same settings in the same order, and the evaluator's fit equals the recorded one to a relative 1e-6 (f0 50,131.18 ± 95.45 Hz, Q 1.4749 ± 0.0152, reduced chi-square 4,834). No protocol was changed for this; the transient protocol is not replayed, because its records are in the archive only.
- Both LED runs, whole. The evaluation equals the recorded one to a relative 1e-6, the characteristic and the held-out check included.

The tolerance is the product's own for a replay on another machine (`agent_campaign.TOLERANCE`). On the machine that recorded the runs (macOS) the replay gives the recorded numbers exactly. On CI's Linux the fits differ from them by up to 3e-8 relative, in the fitted values and in their standard errors, which come from a finite-difference Jacobian.

The measurement tables agree to 1e-9. The live run read them from the device's samples at full precision, and the replay reads `raw.csv`, which keeps 12 significant digits.
